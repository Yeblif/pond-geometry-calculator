Pond base gradients

The base follows the ground, so the excavation depth is the same everywhere (minimum depth = centre depth). Water is still level and limited by the lowest rim point, so on sloping ground the high end stores less usable water.

Base gradients are independent of ground. Depth is always the minimum vertical distance between the ground plane and base plane anywhere in the top footprint.

Freeboard is measured vertically below the lowest rim elevation (overflow point), not the highest rim. Water is always horizontal. Where the base or pond sides rise above the water level, they store no water.

Permanent water level

The outlet invert of a wet pond. Water below it is the permanent (treatment) volume; storage between it and the design water level is the attenuation volume.

Export DXF saves the top rim, estimated base toe and design waterline as AutoCAD 3D polylines on separate layers, at real levels (datum + relative level), in metres. XY is local with the pond centre at 0,0, unless the outline was imported from a drawing, in which case the original coordinates are kept. Toe and waterline contours are sampled approximations, not setting-out geometry.

Model: a planar ground (rim) surface and planar base with independent X/Y grades. Depth is the minimum vertical ground-to-base separation anywhere in the top footprint; the actual centre excavation depth is adjusted to maintain that minimum. Sides drop from their closest top-rim point at the specified inward slope, meeting the base where possible. The pond cannot store water above its lowest rim point; freeboard is below that controlling spill elevation. Maximum storage is not an earthworks excavation quantity. Numerical grid integration and rendered meshes are approximate; finite sampling can affect narrow shapes and steep slopes. Flat zero-gradient results correspond to a conventional level-rim pond. No outfall, berm/retaining-wall design, ground-condition, hydraulic routing, sediment or operational storage allowance is included. Concept sizing only; verify detailed levels, stability, and volumes in CAD.
